Standards and checks

Design values are never hidden in the code. They live in standard files, every check tells you which value it used and where it comes from, and a design can record justified deviations. The Nepal Road Standard 2070 (second revision, Department of Roads) ships as the default standard, transcribed table by table from the printed document with its clause and table references.

What NRS 2070 fixes. Road classes I to IV by traffic (Table 24-1), terrain by cross slope (Table 6-1), design speeds (Table 7-1), sight distances (Tables 8-1, 8-2), minimum radii with and without superelevation and for comfort (Table 9-1), transition lengths by radius (Table 9-2) and the 1000 m / 0.25 m shift rules (cl. 9.2), hair-pin bends (Table 9-3), extra widening (Table 9-4), set-back distance (cl. 9.5), maximum gradients by speed with altitude and curve compensation (Table 10-1, cl. 10.1.2), critical lengths of grade (Table 10-2), K values (Tables 10-3, 10-4), lane and shoulder widths (Tables 11-1, 11-2, 24-1), camber (Table 11-3), superelevation limits and runoff (cl. 11.6), embankment slopes by height and cut slopes by material (Tables 11-4, 11-5), right of way (Table 11-6), drainage rules (cl. 13.8) and the clothoid aesthetics of cl. 19.

Hover a check to see the source and the status of its parameter. The Excel workbook lists every check with the same information on its Checks sheet.

Design parameters select the rows

Standards give different values for different roads. The Design parameters card of the Alignment stage sets the functional road class (I expressway, II arterial, III collector, IV local, by 20-year traffic in PCU/day), the terrain (plain, rolling, mountainous or steep by the cross slope of the country), the design speed, the pavement surface, the cut material of Table 11-5 and the administrative road type (National Highway, Feeder Road, District Road) that fixes the right of way. Every check and the default typical section use the matching table rows. Change a parameter and the checks re-evaluate on the next save.

Design speed. Leave the speed on standard to use Table 7-1 for the class and terrain; the standard allows 75 % of that value in very difficult terrain, which you enter explicitly. Side friction, sight distance, gradient and K limits are looked up for the chosen speed.
Snow-bound and altitude. Tick snow-bound area to cap the superelevation at 7 % (cl. 11.6 b). Ease gradients for altitude lowers the maximum gradient by 0.5 % for every 500 m of the design's mean level above sea level (cl. 10.1.2 a); switch it off for projects whose levels are not above mean sea level.
Older designs. Designs created with the earlier class names are mapped automatically: National Highway to class II, Feeder Road to class III, District and Village Roads to class IV; the old material names map to the Table 11-5 categories.

defaultAn application default where the standard gives no number: cut-slope berm height and width, the minimum vertical curve length (NRS works with K values), the fill height and cut depth that trigger wall suggestions, and the usual height and span ranges of each wall, drain and culvert type.Adjust in the templates or the structure record, or record a deviation; the checks name them as defaults.

Maintaining a standard (administrators)

Standards are JSON files in the plm/design/standards folder of the installation; nrs-2070.json holds the Nepal Road Standard 2070. Each parameter table has rows keyed by road class, terrain, speed, radius, gradient, fill height, surface or material (a * row applies to all), and every value carries its unit, source (clause or table) and status. Numeric keys are matched to the nearest not-larger row, to the nearest not-smaller row when the table says "lookup": "ceil", or interpolated when it says "interpolate": true. To change a value, edit the figure, cite the clause in source and keep the status; to add a standard, copy the file under a new id and choose it in the design settings. The option lists of the Design parameters card (classes, terrains, materials, road types) come from the file's meta block, so a new standard can use its own vocabulary.

Responsibility. The software applies the values it is given. The engineer signing the design remains responsible for using the correct edition of the standard and for every deviation.
